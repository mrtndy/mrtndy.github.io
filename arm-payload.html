Printed links bend under load, so sag is calculated. Planned: cancel it in the controller.
[ why sag? read more ]

The links are 3D printed and the joint parts are CNC machined, driven by planetary-geared BLDC motors. Printed links bend under load, so the hand sits lower than the joint angles alone say. Sag has to be considered when the arm is actuated, so it is calculated here next to the payload.

Plan: remove it with control algorithms. The controller predicts the sag for each posture and payload and shifts the joint targets to cancel it. The prediction starts from this stiffness model. A bench test with known weights then gives one measured stiffness per joint, which also takes in the gearboxes and bearings. Not built yet.

This view needs WebGL, which this browser did not provide.
The numbers: 1.5 kg held continuously and 5.8 kg lifted for a few seconds, in any posture (calculated).
